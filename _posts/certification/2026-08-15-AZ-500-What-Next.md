# AZ-500 is retiring. What actually happens next?

Microsoft is retiring **Exam AZ-500: Microsoft Azure Security Technologies** and the **Microsoft Certified: Azure Security Engineer Associate** certification on **August 31, 2026**.[[1]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-security-engineer/)

Now the important part: retirement is not the same thing as revocation.

 What changes is what you can do **going forward**.

Your AZ-500 credential can remain valid after the retirement date. What disappears is the ability to earn it again or keep renewing it forever.[[2]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/credential-retirement)

That distinction matters.

---

## The three clocks you need to understand

There are three different dates people tend to mix together:

1. **Exam retirement date** — August 31, 2026.
2. **Certification retirement date** — also August 31, 2026.
3. **Your personal certification expiration date** — shown in your Microsoft Learn profile.

The first two are global. The third one is yours.

Microsoft says that after a renewable certification is retired, an already-earned credential remains in the **Active Certifications** section of your transcript until it reaches its own expiration date. After that, it moves to your historical certifications.[[2]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/credential-retirement)

So if your AZ-500 says it expires later than August 31, 2026, the retirement date does **not** magically shorten the certificate to August 31.

Your credential remains active until the expiration date shown on the credential.

## What happens if I can renew AZ-500 right now?

**Renew it before retirement if keeping the credential active longer is useful to you.**

Microsoft role-based certifications normally open their renewal window six months before expiration. Renewal is free and is done through the online Microsoft Learn renewal assessment.[[3]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification-faq)

There is one extra constraint now:

> **AZ-500 renewal itself retires on August 31, 2026.**
> 

Microsoft explicitly says the certification, exam, **and renewal assessments** retire on that date. Afterward, you will no longer be able to earn or renew AZ-500.[[1]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-security-engineer/)

If the Renew button is available in your Learn profile, do not treat August 31 as a theoretical deadline. Do it before then.

When you successfully renew a Microsoft certification, Microsoft extends it **one year from the existing expiration date**, not one year from the day you clicked Renew.[[4]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification)

### Example

Suppose your AZ-500 expires on **November 15, 2026**, and you are eligible to renew before retirement.

You renew on **August 20, 2026**.

Your certification can then be extended by one year from its current expiration date, so it can remain active well beyond the August 31 retirement date.

But once AZ-500 is retired, there is no second renewal cycle waiting for you next year.

That last renewal is effectively the end of the AZ-500 lifecycle for you.

## What if my AZ-500 expires after retirement but I am not eligible to renew yet?

Then there may be nothing to do for AZ-500 itself.

Microsoft only allows renewal during the eligibility window, and a retiring certification can no longer be renewed after its retirement date. Microsoft notes that retirement can affect the renewal window and that the **Renew by** messaging in Learn takes the retirement date into account.[[3]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification-faq)

The practical rule is simple:

**Open Microsoft Learn → Profile → Certifications and trust the renewal date shown there.**

If AZ-500 gives you a renewal option before August 31, use it if you want the extra validity period.

If it does not, you cannot manufacture a renewal by retaking AZ-500. Microsoft says retaking the certification exam is not an alternative way to renew a certification.[[3]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification-faq)

## Does my AZ-500 automatically become SC-500?

**No.**

There is no credential rewrite where:

`AZ-500 → SC-500`

Your existing Azure Security Engineer Associate credential remains its own credential until it expires.

Microsoft's new forward path is:

**Microsoft Certified: Cloud and AI Security Engineer Associate**  

**Exam: SC-500 — Implementing End-to-End Security Controls for Cloud and AI Workloads**

Microsoft's transition guidance tells learners who are not already close to taking AZ-500 to prepare for and take **SC-500 instead**.[[5]](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/skills-hub-blog/the-ai-job-boom-is-here-are-you-ready-to-showcase-your-skills/4494128)

There is currently **no automatic upgrade credit, conversion, or bridge exam** that turns an AZ-500 holder into an SC-500 holder.

If you want the new credential on your profile, you need to earn **SC-500 separately**.

## Is SC-500 just AZ-500 with a new number?

No — and this is the interesting part.

AZ-500 was primarily about securing Azure infrastructure: identity, networking, compute, storage, databases, Defender for Cloud, and Sentinel.

SC-500 keeps a large part of that foundation, but the security boundary is wider.

Microsoft describes the new role as protecting systems and data across **Azure, hybrid, and AI-enabled environments**. It explicitly adds responsibility for securing the platforms, identities, data, infrastructure, and agents used by AI workloads.[[6]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/cloud-and-ai-security-engineer-associate/)

The SC-500 blueprint is currently organized around four domains:[[7]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/sc-500)

- **Manage identity, access, and governance — 20–25%**
- **Secure storage, databases, and networking — 25–30%**
- **Secure compute — 20–25%**
- **Manage and monitor security posture — 20–25%**

And inside those domains Microsoft now includes topics such as:

- Microsoft Entra ID, PIM, Conditional Access, managed identities, and Key Vault.
- Azure Policy, Defender for Cloud, regulatory compliance, RBAC, and infrastructure as code.
- Azure networking, Private Link, Firewall, storage, and database security.
- AKS, containers, App Service, Functions, APIs, and hybrid/multicloud server security.
- **AI workload security**, including Microsoft Foundry controls, AI Gateway, Defender for AI, Entra Agent ID, Copilot-related risk, and agent security.
- Microsoft Sentinel and **Microsoft Security Copilot**.

So the mental model is:

> **AZ-500 asks: can you secure Azure?**
> 

> **SC-500 asks: can you secure the modern cloud estate — including AI workloads — end to end?**
> 

That is why I would not think of SC-500 as a simple rename. It is an expansion of the security engineer role.

## What should an existing AZ-500 holder do?

| Situation | Recommended move |
| --- | --- |
| Your AZ-500 is active and Renew is available | Renew before August 31, 2026 if the additional active period is valuable to you. |
| Your AZ-500 is active but Renew is not available | Keep using the credential until its displayed expiration date. Start preparing for SC-500. |
| Your AZ-500 expires before August 31, 2026 | Renew before your personal expiration date. Your own expiry is the earlier deadline. |
| You want the current Microsoft cloud-security credential | Plan to earn SC-500 separately. AZ-500 does not automatically convert. |
| You are studying AZ-500 but have not booked/taken it | Unless you specifically need AZ-500 before retirement and are ready now, move your study plan to SC-500. |

## My recommendation

If you already hold AZ-500, do **two separate things**:

1. **Preserve the old signal if it is cheap to preserve.** If Microsoft Learn gives you the AZ-500 renewal assessment, take it before retirement. It is free, and passing it can extend the credential from its current expiry date.[[4]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification)
2. **Build the next signal instead of clinging to the old one.** Start mapping your AZ-500 knowledge to SC-500, especially the areas that are genuinely new: AI workload security, Security Copilot, agent identity/security, broader application-platform security, and modern Defender controls.[[7]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/sc-500)

Do not think of this as Microsoft deleting the work you already did.

Think of it as a protocol migration.

Your old certificate proves what you knew and when you proved it. It remains on your transcript. The new certification is Microsoft saying, *the security engineer boundary has moved; here is the new contract.*

## FAQ

### Will AZ-500 disappear from my Microsoft transcript on August 31, 2026?

No. An earned certification remains on your transcript. For a renewable role-based certification such as AZ-500, it remains active until its own expiration date, then moves to the historical section.[[2]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/credential-retirement)

### Can I renew AZ-500 after August 31, 2026?

No. Microsoft says the certification and its renewal assessments retire on August 31, 2026.[[1]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-security-engineer/)

### Can I renew by simply retaking AZ-500?

No. Microsoft says the renewal assessment is the renewal mechanism; retaking the certification exam is not an alternative renewal path.[[3]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification-faq)

### Will Microsoft upgrade my AZ-500 to SC-500 automatically?

No automatic conversion has been announced. SC-500 is a separate certification with its own exam and expanded objectives.[[6]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/cloud-and-ai-security-engineer-associate/)

### Is SC-500 available now?

Yes. As of this article's **August 11, 2026** check, Microsoft Learn lists **Microsoft Certified: Cloud and AI Security Engineer Associate** and provides the option to schedule Exam SC-500.[[6]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/cloud-and-ai-security-engineer-associate/)

### If I already know AZ-500, am I starting from zero for SC-500?

Knowledge-wise, absolutely not. There is substantial overlap in identity, network, compute, storage, Defender for Cloud, governance, and monitoring. Credential-wise, however, SC-500 is earned separately. Your best strategy is to reuse your AZ-500 foundation and focus study time on the expanded SC-500 objectives.[[7]](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/sc-500)
